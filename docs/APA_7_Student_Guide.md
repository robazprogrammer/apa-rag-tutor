# APA 7 Student Guide

An original classroom quick guide. The examples use invented names and titles.

## Student title page, page numbers, and running head

For a full APA 7 student paper, put the page number at the top right, starting with 1 on the title page. Center the title in bold in the upper half of the page, about three or four lines below the top margin. Below it, center your name, department and school, course number and name, instructor's name, and assignment due date. Double-space the page and leave one blank double-spaced line between the title and author name. A student paper normally uses the page number alone in its header. Include a running head if the instructor or institution requires one.

Source: https://apastyle.apa.org/instructional-aids/student-paper-setup-guide.pdf

## Margins, font, double spacing, and paragraph indent

Set one-inch margins on each side. Use a readable font consistently, such as 11-point Calibri, 11-point Arial, or 12-point Times New Roman. Double-space ordinary paper text and the reference list. Align paragraphs at the left margin and indent the first line of a regular body paragraph by half an inch. Leave the right margin ragged. Avoid inserting extra blank lines between regular paragraphs.

Source: https://apastyle.apa.org/instructional-aids/student-paper-setup-guide.pdf

## First body page and abstract

At the top of the first page of the body, repeat the full paper title in bold and center it. Start the introduction beneath the title. For an APA student paper, an abstract is usually included only when requested by the instructor or institution. If your assignment specifies an abstract or a different structure, apply those directions.

Source: https://apastyle.apa.org/instructional-aids/beginner-student-paper-checklist.pdf

## Paper headings and heading levels

A Level 1 heading is centered, bold, and in title case. A Level 2 heading is left-aligned, bold, and in title case. A Level 3 heading is left-aligned, bold italic, and in title case. The text starts on a new line after each of those levels. Start with Level 1 when you need a heading, then use Level 2 for a subsection and Level 3 for a subsection within it. The paper title serves as the introduction heading, so the introduction usually has no separate heading labeled Introduction.

Source: https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/paper-format/headings

## Discussion post, Canvas, and assignment directions

Check the prompt for length, required sources, heading or title page requirements, and how to submit. When it asks for APA citations and references in a discussion post, place author–date citations beside the borrowed ideas and include the corresponding references in the form your instructor requests. A forum response may have a different layout from a full student paper. For example, if your instructor asks for citations and a reference list and explicitly says to skip the title page, follow those directions for that submission. If the prompt is unclear, ask whether it requires a paper layout or citation and reference formatting only.

Source: https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/paper-format/sample-papers

## In-text citation (parenthetical and narrative) for one, two, or three or more authors

An in-text citation appears in your sentence, not in the reference list. A parenthetical citation puts the author and year inside parentheses. A narrative citation names the author in your sentence and puts the year in parentheses. For one author, use the surname and year, for example (Rivera, 2024). In a narrative citation, write Rivera (2024). For two authors, cite both surnames each time. Use an ampersand in parentheses, as in (Rivera & Chen, 2024), and spell out “and” in the sentence. For three or more authors, normally use only the first surname followed by “et al.” and the year, including on first mention. Examples are (Rivera et al., 2024) and Rivera et al. (2024). When this would make two different works look identical, include enough surnames to distinguish them. The rule for listing authors in the reference list is separate.

Source: https://apastyle.apa.org/instructional-aids/beginner-student-paper-checklist.pdf

## Paraphrase and page number

For a paraphrase, use your own wording and credit the author and year. A page number is optional for a paraphrase. It can help a reader locate a particular idea in a long book or report. For an example, write (Rivera, 2024) or, with an optional page locator, (Rivera, 2024, p. 18). An instructor may request page numbers for paraphrases in a particular course.

Source: https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/citations/paraphrasing

## Direct quote, block quote, and page locator

When you directly quote a source, cite the author, year, and a page or other locator. Use p. for one page and pp. for a page range. When pages are unavailable, use a paragraph number, section heading, or another suitable locator such as a video timestamp. Put quotes of fewer than 40 words in quotation marks within the paragraph. For 40 words or more, start a block on its own line, indent the whole block half an inch, double-space it, and omit quotation marks. When the citation follows a block quote, place it after the closing punctuation.

For three or more authors, normally use the first surname plus “et al.” in a direct-quote citation. For example, after the closing punctuation of a block quote from page 27 of a 2023 work by Lee, Santos, and Okafor, write (Lee et al., 2023, p. 27).

Source: https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/citations/quotations/index

## Reference list heading, alphabetization, and hanging indent

Begin the reference list on a fresh page. Center the bold heading References. Put entries in alphabetical order by the first author's surname, double-space them, and apply a half-inch hanging indent. A hanging indent keeps the first line at the left margin and moves later lines to the right. In most cases, each in-text citation needs a matching reference entry and each reference entry should appear as a citation in the paper. Personal communications generally appear only in text.

Source: https://apastyle.apa.org/instructional-aids/beginner-student-paper-checklist.pdf

## Journal article reference and DOI

Use this sequence for a journal article: author surnames and initials, publication year, article title, journal title, volume, issue when available, page range or article number, and DOI when available. Put the article title in sentence case. Italicize the journal name and volume. Keep the issue number in parentheses in regular type. Present the DOI as a URL beginning with https://doi.org/ and omit a period after it. For an article from a typical academic database with no DOI, end after the publication details.

Source: https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/references/examples/journal-article-references

## Webpage reference, organization author, and missing date

For a standalone webpage reference, list the person or organization responsible, date, italicized page title, site name, and page URL. When the author and website name are the same, leave out the site name. If no publication date is available, use (n.d.). Retrieval dates are generally for content meant to change over time when earlier versions are unavailable. If the source is an article, report, or other named work hosted online, look up the reference format for that source type.

Source: https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/references/examples/webpage-website-references

## Book reference

For an authored book, list the author's surname and initials, year in parentheses, italicized book title in sentence case, publisher, and DOI if available. If the book has a later edition, put the edition in regular type within parentheses after the title. Omit the publisher's city. A chapter in an edited book uses its own format.

Source: https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/references/examples/book-references
